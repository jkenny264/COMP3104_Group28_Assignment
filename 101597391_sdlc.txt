DevOps in the Software Development Lifecycle
Contributor: Jocelyn Brown
Student ID: 101597391

Overview:

The software development lifecycle includes planning, requirements
analysis, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance.
Teams may revisit these activities as requirements change and feedback
reveals opportunities for improvement.

DevOps supports collaboration throughout this lifecycle by connecting
development and operational responsibilities. Teams consider how software
will be tested, released, monitored, and maintained while designing
and implementing it.

Version control provides a shared history of changes. Automation makes
repeatable tasks more consistent, while feedback helps teams identify
problems and decide what to improve next.

Continuous Integration and Early Feedback:

Continuous integration encourages developers to integrate changes
frequently and check them automatically. Depending on the project,
a pipeline can run tests, check code style, and verify that the
application builds successfully.

Early feedback helps developers identify problems while the related
changes are still small and easier to investigate. Teams can use
pipeline results alongside code reviews to decide whether a change
is ready to merge.

Automated checks support quality, but passing checks do not prove
that software is free of defects. Their usefulness depends on what
they test, so teams still need thoughtful reviews and appropriate
manual testing.
